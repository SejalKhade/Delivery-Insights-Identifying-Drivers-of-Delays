On-Time Rate by Market

Market 1 lags every other region by a structural gap, not just volume spikes

Finding: Even after controlling for cuisine mix and order size, Market 1 underperforms Market 5 (p < 0.001). OR ≈ 0.50 at baseline — a structural gap, not noise.

Delivery Delay Distribution

Most deliveries cluster near 0 min delay; the tail extends well past 30 min

Threshold: 20-minute delay chosen as "late" — reflects 2015 customer expectations and operational variability.

On-Time Rate by Hour of Day

Late-night and dinner rush hours show the sharpest performance drops

Finding: 2–3 AM hits lowest rates (7.5%). Dinner rush (3–8 PM) also underperforms. Pre-positioning dashers before these windows is the primary lever.

On-Time Rate by Cuisine Type

Cuisine prep variability explains a significant share of the performance gap

Finding: Brazilian and Nepalese cuisines have the lowest on-time rates. Smoothies and breakfast perform best — predictable prep times matter.

A/B Test Simulation — Intervention Outcomes (Market 1)

Two simulated interventions tested against control — neither reached significance alone

Key takeaway: Reducing busy_ratio alone (p=0.407) or order-to-dasher ratio alone (p=0.447) did not significantly improve on-time rates. Single-lever fixes don't work — combined operational changes (more dashers + improved batching + cuisine-specific prep agreements) are needed.
